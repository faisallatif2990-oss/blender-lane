# Skill #87 — The living exhibit

**Route:** plain route — the claim stands or falls on a living person, not on paper alone. The camera rule binds the whole clip: the person appears in REAL footage only (never a still photo, never a drawn figure, never an AI face).
**Clip:** 1920×1080, ~10.3 s, Hindi VO (locked voice, natural speed — never time-stretched), English on-screen text, clean small captions with gold hot-words, voice + sound from the very first frame, −14 LUFS master.

## What it is

A person whose documented status contradicts reality is presented as a walking exhibit: the official record says one thing (dead, missing, never existed) and the living person is shown moving on screen in the same beat. The contradiction is not argued — it is *shown*, record on one side, breathing human on the other.

## The showcase (fresh original, built for this demo)

- **Line 1** — The record first: a voter-roll page lies full-front, and one row lights up ON the paper — "RAM SARAN S/O BUDDHU — ELECTION ROLL No. 1147" — then a heavy red **DEAD** stamp slams onto the row. *VO (Hindi): "In the government list, this man is dead."*
- **Line 2** — The page slides aside and the man himself walks toward the camera in real footage (public-domain film, credited on screen), while a clean side-box callout beside the frame — leader line to the walking figure — states it plainly: "DECLARED DEAD — WALKING, ALIVE". *VO: "And look — he is walking, alive, in front of the camera."*
- **Line 3** — The verdict lands over the still-walking figure: the paper was wrong, the man is the exhibit. *VO: "The paper was false — the man himself is the proof."*

## Benefit

- The strongest possible refutation format: no narration can out-argue a living person walking on camera.
- Teaches the audience a transferable scepticism — official paper can be wrong — without a lecture.
- The side-by-side (record vs person) makes the contradiction legible in one glance, even muted.

## Where it is used

"Declared dead" pension and voter-roll cases, missing-person stories where the person resurfaces, identity disputes, and any segment where a file claims something a living human disproves by existing.

## Honest limitations

- **Stand-in disclosure:** the walking man in this demo is a genuine period pedestrian from Edison's public-domain actuality *What Happened on Twenty-third Street* (1901, Library of Congress via archive.org — full credit in `assets/CREDIT.txt` and on screen). He is not Ram Saran; he proves the *staging* of the technique. A production clip must use footage of the actual person, obtained with consent.
- The named case (Ram Saran) is an invented composite for the demo; the pattern it shows — men declared dead on paper while alive — is a documented real-world phenomenon, but no real person's record is reproduced here.
- The footage is a silent-era actuality, so it reads as "a man walking" rather than a modern interview; the technique's full force needs present-day footage of the real person speaking.
