# Skill #86 — The AI panel

**Route:** 99-style 67 (Dark Keynote) — one dark stage, one accent, cards bloom one by one.
**Clip:** 1920×1080, ~12.4 s, Hindi VO (locked voice, natural speed — never time-stretched), English on-screen text, clean small captions with gold hot-words, voice + sound from the very first frame, −14 LUFS master.

## What it is

The same question is put to several independent AI models, and the chorus — or the split — is shown on screen as data, not claimed in narration. The technique borrows the borrowed-authority power of a judge's bench or a scientific consensus: instead of "trust me", the video shows six named systems answering side by side, and the audience watches agreement (or disagreement) happen.

## The showcase (fresh original, built for this demo)

- **Line 1** — The method is declared while the stage assembles: a single question card rises — "HISTORY VAULT'S QUESTION: 'CAN THIS CLAIM BE PUBLISHED AS FACT?'" — and six empty labelled slots draw themselves beneath it. *VO (Hindi): "This claim was not asked of one, but of six AI systems."*
- **Line 2** — The six panels bloom in a 3×2 grid — CHATGPT, GEMINI, DEEPSEEK, PERPLEXITY, CLAUDE, GROK — each one *answering*, one by one: "PUBLISH IT". A counter at the top ticks 0/6 → 6/6 as each verdict lands. *VO: "Every single one said the same thing — publish it."*
- **Line 3** — The honest turn, spoken over the frozen chorus: the agreement is not proof, and the narration hands the judgement back to the viewer — "NOW JUDGE IT YOURSELF". *VO: "But agreement is not proof — so judge it yourself."*

## Benefit

- Gives a claim a jury instead of a narrator: consensus reads as verification, and a split reads as a warning — both are information the audience can use.
- Six named systems answering on camera is far harder to fake in the viewer's mind than a voiceover assertion.
- The counter makes the chorus countable: agreement becomes a number, not an adjective.

## Where it is used

Contested claims where the channel's own judgement would look biased — "did Alexander's army really reach the Beas?", AI-checked translations of inscriptions, dating disputes. Also as a pre-verdict beat before the channel states its own position: the panel goes first, the host second.

## Honest limitations

- AI agreement is not evidence; the showcase says so on screen (line 3) and every real use must keep that line — the panel is a chorus, never a source. The underlying proof still has to come from the documents.
- The six answers in this demo are performed (typed by the build script), not captured live from the services; a production use must record real answers, or label the panel as a reconstruction.
- Six identical verdicts flatten nuance: where models genuinely split, the split must be shown honestly, including the minority answer.

## Build (on the lane)

`bash build.sh` → renders `s86.mp4` + `s86_review.mp4`, prints the QC line. Needs only python3 + Pillow + numpy and ffmpeg. The locked-voice VO ships inside the bundle (`s86_vo_0..2.mp3`); no TTS or network is used on the lane.
