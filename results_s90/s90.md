# Skill #90 — The definitional verdict

**Route:** motionmaxxing — definition cards, clause-by-clause matching choreography.
**Clip:** 1920×1080, ~13.3 s, Hindi VO (locked voice, natural speed — never time-stretched), English on-screen text, clean small captions with gold hot-words, voice + sound from the very first frame, −14 LUFS master.

## What it is

A contested label ("arrested"? "detained"? "rescued"?) is settled not by argument but by definition: the candidate words are defined on screen, clause by clause, and the established facts are checked against every clause in the open. The word that survives all its clauses wins; the words whose clauses fail are crossed out one by one, in view. The verdict is then just the last word standing — the audience watched it earn the place.

## The showcase (fresh original, built for this demo)

- **Line 1** — The two official candidate words take the stage as definition cards, and their clauses are read onto them: DETAINED — "HELD BRIEFLY" · "PAPERWORK FILED" · "FAMILY INFORMED"; ARRESTED — "FORMAL CHARGE" · "PRODUCED BEFORE A COURT" · "RECORD KEPT". *VO (Hindi): "The government used two words — detained, and arrested."*
- **Line 2** — The facts arrive as a strip and are checked against every clause: "HELD 41 DAYS · NO CHARGE FILED · FAMILY INFORMED ON DAY 39 · NO COURT, NO RECORD". Red crosses stack up on clause after clause — both official words fail, visibly, one clause at a time. *VO: "But the facts matched neither word."*
- **Line 3** — Both failed cards crumble into falling shards, and the word the facts *do* fit lands letter by letter in the empty frame: "KIDNAPPED". *VO: "The word that fit the facts was a third one — kidnapped."*

## Benefit

- Converts a shouting-match label dispute into a procedure the viewer can re-run on any story: define, then check every clause.
- The clause-by-clause crosses make the failure *countable* — six small verdicts carry more weight than one big assertion.
- The crumbling cards give the verdict a physical finality that a voiceover adjective cannot.

## Where it is used

Official-language disputes — "encounter" vs killing, "evacuation" vs expulsion, "martyr" vs casualty, "reform" vs cut — and any segment where an institution's chosen word is doing quiet political work.

## Honest limitations

- The definitions must be real (dictionary/legal) and quoted fairly; a rigged definition makes the whole procedure propaganda. Production use should cite the definition source in the description.
- The case in this demo is an invented composite (no real incident is depicted); the fact strip's four facts are illustrative, chosen so both words genuinely fail.
- The verdict word is deliberately grave; the technique must be reserved for cases where every clause truly fails — used loosely once, it loses its force forever.

## Build (on the lane)

`bash build.sh` → renders `s90.mp4` + `s90_review.mp4`, prints the QC line. Needs only python3 + Pillow + numpy and ffmpeg. The locked-voice VO ships inside the bundle (`s90_vo_0..2.mp3`); no TTS or network is used on the lane.
